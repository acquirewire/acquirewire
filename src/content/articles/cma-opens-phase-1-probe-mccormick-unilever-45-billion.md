## What Happened

The UK Competition and Markets Authority has launched a Phase 1 investigation into McCormick's planned combination with Unilever's foods business, the regulator said on 16 September. The CMA must decide by 11 November whether the deal could result in a substantial lessening of competition in any UK market, or whether to refer it for an in-depth Phase 2 review. The regulator had invited comments on 21 July, with responses due by 5 August.

The transaction, announced in March, values Unilever's food operations at about $45 billion and would combine brands including Knorr and Hellmann's with McCormick's French's mustard and Frank's RedHot. McCormick will pay $15.7 billion in cash, with Unilever shareholders owning 55% of the combined company and Unilever itself retaining a 9.9% stake. The companies expect about $300 million of annual cost savings and completion by mid-2027. Unilever has already put its Colman's mustard brand up for sale to address the obvious overlap with French's.

## Why It Matters

**Mustard is the tell, but sauces are the substance.** Colman's and French's together would have dominated UK mustard, which is why Unilever moved pre-emptively to sell. The harder questions concern condiments, cooking sauces and seasonings more broadly, where Hellmann's, Knorr and McCormick's herbs-and-spices range each hold strong positions and retailers will argue about shelf leverage.

**A Phase 2 referral would push the timetable.** The companies have targeted a mid-2027 close. A full in-depth review typically adds around six months and could bring divestiture demands beyond Colman's. Even a Phase 1 clearance with undertakings would set the template for other regulators reviewing the same combination.

**The deal is central to both companies' strategies.** For Unilever, exiting food completes its shift to beauty, personal care and home products. For McCormick, the combination transforms a spice company into a global flavour and sauces group with a 55% Unilever-shareholder base. Neither side can afford a prolonged regulatory stall.
