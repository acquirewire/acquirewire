## What Happened

The Baldwin Group has agreed to be taken private through a majority investment by Sequence Holdings and DFO Management, the Dell family office, in an all-cash transaction with a total enterprise value of approximately $7.7 billion, the company announced on 14 September. Shareholders will receive $32.50 per share, an 88% premium to the unaffected close on 17 June, the day before reports first emerged that Baldwin was weighing a sale.

The enterprise value comprises an equity purchase price of about $4.6 billion and roughly $3.1 billion of net debt to be assumed or refinanced. Eligible Baldwin colleagues will retain a significant minority equity stake alongside the new owners. The deal is expected to close in the first quarter of 2027. Management framed the transaction as giving the broker flexibility to pursue long-term investments, including in AI, without the pressure of quarterly earnings and share-price swings.

Analysts at TD Cowen described Sequence and DFO as "ideal owners" for the business, according to The Insurer. Baldwin, formerly known as BRP Group, is one of the fastest-growing independent brokers in the United States and has expanded largely through acquisition — a strategy that built scale but also the debt that weighed on its public valuation.

## Why It Matters

**An 88% premium is a statement about public-market mispricing.** Baldwin's shares had languished under the weight of its debt load and integration questions from years of acquisitions. Sequence and DFO are betting that a leveraged, still-growing brokerage platform is worth far more to a patient owner than to a public market fixated on quarterly leverage ratios.

**Insurance brokerage is the sector's consolidation hotspot.** The deal lands two weeks after Aon's $17 billion purchase of USI and amid a steady flow of sponsor-backed roll-ups. Brokerage cash flows are recurring, capital-light and resilient to the underwriting cycle, which is why family offices and private-equity firms keep paying up for scale platforms.

**Employee ownership is the retention mechanism.** Keeping colleagues invested matters more in brokerage than almost any other industry, because client relationships sit with individual producers. The rollover stake is designed to prevent the talent flight that has undermined other broker take-privates.
