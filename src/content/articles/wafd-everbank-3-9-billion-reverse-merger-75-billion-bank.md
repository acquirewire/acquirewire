## What Happened

WaFd has entered into a $3.9 billion reverse merger with Florida-based EverBank Financial Corp, the companies announced on 7 September, creating a regional lender with roughly $75 billion in assets. Under the structure, EverBank merges into WaFd, which remains the publicly traded entity but will rename itself EverBank Financial Corp and trade on Nasdaq under the ticker EVBK.

EverBank's investors will own about 59.2% of the combined company, with WaFd shareholders holding the remaining 40.8%. WaFd said it expects earnings-per-share accretion of approximately 29% in 2027 for its shareholders, with a tangible-book-value earn-back period of under two years, and described the deal as strengthening its position for long-term growth and improved profitability. Management hosted an investor call at 5am Pacific time on 8 September.

EverBank, headquartered in Jacksonville, Florida, brings a nationally oriented deposit and lending franchise; WaFd, based in Seattle, is a long-established Western thrift with a branch network across several states. The Wall Street Journal reported the combination on 7 September, the Labor Day holiday, ahead of the formal announcement.

## Why It Matters

**This is a listing for EverBank in all but name.** Reverse mergers let a larger private institution access public markets through a smaller listed partner without the cost and uncertainty of an IPO. EverBank gets a Nasdaq ticker and a currency for further deals; WaFd shareholders get a minority stake in a bigger, more diversified bank and a sharp accretion number to justify giving up control.

**Regional-bank consolidation is back on.** A $75 billion balance sheet puts the combined group squarely in the mid-tier that regulators and investors have been encouraging to scale up. Combining WaFd's Pacific Northwest and Western footprint with EverBank's Southeast and national digital franchise creates geographic diversity neither had alone.

**The accretion promise sets a high bar.** A 29% EPS uplift with a sub-two-year earn-back is unusually strong for a bank deal and implies substantial cost savings. Delivering it will require integrating two very different institutions, a traditional branch-based thrift and a digitally oriented lender, without losing deposits or key people.
