## What Happened

KKR has made an approximately $9 billion takeover bid for UGI Corporation, offering $42.50 a share in cash for the Pennsylvania-based natural-gas and electricity distributor, according to a Wall Street Journal report picked up by Bloomberg. The price represents a 21% premium to UGI's $35.09 close on 17 August, and the stock jumped more than 11% on the news while KKR's own shares slipped roughly 1.3%.

The proposal includes a 30-day "go-shop" period, allowing UGI's board to canvass rival offers before committing — a structure that signals KKR expects the board to test the market and is comfortable with the risk of being outbid. UGI's businesses span regulated gas and electric utilities alongside propane distribution, a mix that has been overlooked by public-market investors for years.

## Why It Matters

**Sleepy utilities have become AI-era infrastructure plays.** Surging demand for reliable power from data-centre construction has turned previously unloved natural-gas utilities into takeover targets. A regulated distributor with a physical pipeline footprint in the US Northeast is precisely the kind of asset that benefits when hyperscalers need firm, dispatchable energy — and KKR is paying for that optionality before the public market has fully priced it in.

**The go-shop is a tell about valuation.** A 21% premium on a stock that had drifted for years is meaningful but not extravagant. By including a go-shop, KKR is effectively conceding that other infrastructure-focused buyers — from rival sponsors to strategic utilities — may see more value. Whether anyone tops $42.50 will be the clearest read on how aggressively capital is chasing gas infrastructure right now.

**Regulatory approval will be the long pole.** Utility take-privates run through state public-utility commissions, which scrutinise leverage, rate impacts and service commitments. Pennsylvania regulators have a track record of extracting concessions from financial buyers, and any deal will likely take well over a year to close even without a competing bid.
