## What Happened

Banca Monte dei Paschi di Siena has launched separate all-share offers for Banco BPM and Banca Generali worth a combined €34 billion, or about $40 billion, in a defensive manoeuvre against Intesa Sanpaolo's hostile approach. The bids value Banco BPM at €25.3 billion and Banca Generali at €8.7 billion, according to Bloomberg. MPS's board approved the dual exchange offers on 20 August and unveiled them the following day.

Chief executive Luigi Lovaglio is seeking to forge Italy's third-largest lender rather than accept Intesa's €36 billion cash-and-share offer, which has already raised competition concerns in Rome. To sweeten the pitch to its own shareholders, MPS proposed an extraordinary distribution worth around €4 billion: €1 billion in cash plus Generali shares representing roughly 4.5% of the insurer, drawn from the 13.3% stake MPS holds through Mediobanca.

## Why It Matters

**This is a break-up defence dressed as expansion.** Intesa's plan would carve MPS up: Mediobanca stays with the group, while half of MPS's branches, its Siena headquarters and its brand go to BPER Banca. By proposing to absorb BPM and Banca Generali instead, Lovaglio is arguing the bank is a consolidator, not prey, and daring shareholders to choose growth over Intesa's cash.

**Rome's competition worries are the swing factor.** Intesa is already Italy's largest bank; adding MPS and Mediobanca would concentrate the sector further. MPS's alternative creates a credible third pillar alongside Intesa and UniCredit, which is politically attractive to a government that has spent years nursing MPS back to health.

**Three simultaneous deals is a lot of execution risk.** All-share offers depend on MPS's own valuation holding up. If Intesa raises its bid or Banco BPM's board resists, the arithmetic changes quickly. The Generali stake, now being partly distributed as a sweetener, is the strategic asset every party covets, and its ultimate home will shape Italian finance for a decade.
