## What Happened

Capital Funding Group has closed an $806 million unitranche bridge-to-HUD loan to support the acquisition of a 36-property skilled nursing facility portfolio. The structure is a bridge-to-HUD execution, meaning the borrower intends to refinance into a Department of Housing and Urban Development permanent loan once operational stabilisation allows. At $806 million, it is one of the largest unitranche financings ever completed in the senior care real estate sector.

The source material does not name the buyer or the seller of the 36 facilities, but a deal of this scale almost certainly involves a regional or national nursing home operator and a financial sponsor. CFG, a Baltimore-based specialty finance firm focused on healthcare real estate, is acting as sole lender on the unitranche, absorbing both the senior and mezzanine risk in a single instrument rather than syndicating a traditional senior-plus-mezz stack.

## Why It Matters

**The unitranche structure tells you something about credit conditions right now.** A lender willing to hold $806 million in a single instrument, without syndication, is either highly convicted on the collateral or is pricing the all-in rate aggressively enough to compensate. HUD bridge lending in skilled nursing has historically attracted specialist capital because the eventual government-backed take-out provides a clear exit, but the size of this loan sits well above typical deal execution in the space. It suggests CFG sees a clear path to the HUD permanent financing and is comfortable with the operational risk during the bridge period.

**Skilled nursing is a structurally complex bet.** Occupancy in U.S. skilled nursing facilities has recovered from pandemic lows, but margins remain squeezed by labor costs and Medicaid reimbursement rates that have lagged inflation in many states. A portfolio of 36 facilities will have significant exposure to state-level reimbursement policy. If any material portion of the portfolio is in states with weak Medicaid rate environments, the borrower's ability to stabilise operations fast enough to meet HUD's underwriting thresholds becomes a real question. Delays to the permanent loan take-out extend the bridge period and increase refinancing risk.

**For private credit, this is exactly the territory firms are targeting.** As broadly syndicated loan markets have tightened, specialty finance lenders like CFG have moved aggressively into large single-asset healthcare real estate deals that banks either cannot hold on balance sheet or choose to avoid given regulatory capital treatment of healthcare exposure. An $806 million unitranche executed by a single lender would have been unusual five years ago. Today it fits a pattern of private credit expanding into ever-larger, more operationally complex transactions.
