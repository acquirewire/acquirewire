## What Happened

Marvell Technology and Lumentum Holdings have agreed to merge in an all-stock transaction valued at approximately $115 billion, one of the largest deals in the semiconductor space in years. Marvell shares fell 3% on the news; Lumentum dropped 6%. Coherent, the closest listed peer to Lumentum in optical networking and datacom components, climbed 2%, a classic read-across trade suggesting the market now prices in a tighter supply of independent optical assets.

The all-stock structure means no cash leaves either company at close, but it also means Lumentum shareholders are taking on Marvell equity at a moment when chip valuations remain sensitive to AI spending cycles and export-control risk. Neither company has yet disclosed the exchange ratio or a projected close date, so the implied premium to Lumentum's unaffected share price is not confirmed from available source material.

## Why It Matters

**Scale in the AI infrastructure stack matters more than it did three years ago.** Marvell has spent the past several years repositioning around custom silicon and high-speed interconnects for hyperscale data centres. Lumentum brings laser and optical-component manufacturing that sits directly upstream of the transceivers and co-packaged optics that AI clusters demand in volume. Together, the combined entity would span a broader slice of the data-centre supply chain, from chip design through to the photonics that move data between GPUs at speed.

**All-stock deals in a volatile market carry a specific execution risk.** Because the consideration is entirely in Marvell shares, any deterioration in Marvell's stock between signing and close changes what Lumentum holders actually receive. The 6% drop in Lumentum on day one suggests some shareholders are already discounting either a dilution risk or scepticism about strategic fit. Regulatory review in the US and potentially in China, where both companies have exposure, adds further timeline uncertainty. Antitrust scrutiny of chip-sector mergers has tightened over the past two years, and a deal of this size will attract close attention.

**Coherent's 2% gain tells its own story.** The optical components space has a handful of credible independent players. If Marvell-Lumentum closes, the pool of standalone optical assets shrinks, making Coherent a more obvious target or, at minimum, a stronger negotiating counterparty for supply contracts. That dynamic alone is enough to move a stock 2% on a news day, and it signals that the buy-side is already thinking about second-order consolidation.

**The premium question remains open.** Without a confirmed exchange ratio, it is not possible to say how generous the terms are for Lumentum shareholders. The headline $115 billion figure reflects the combined market capitalisation of both companies, not a standalone acquisition premium. Deals structured this way can obscure the economics until the proxy is filed. Investors in both stocks should watch the ratio carefully when it is disclosed.
