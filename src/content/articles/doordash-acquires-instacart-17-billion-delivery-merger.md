## What Happened

DoorDash has agreed to acquire Instacart in a deal valued at $17 billion, according to multiple reports. The transaction brings together the largest third-party restaurant-delivery network in the United States and the company that has long held the top position in grocery delivery, a category that DoorDash has been pushing into aggressively but had never fully dominated.

Instacart went public in September 2023 at a valuation far below its pandemic-era peak of roughly $39 billion, and the company has been navigating a more competitive and cost-conscious consumer environment ever since. At $17 billion, DoorDash is paying a meaningful premium to where Instacart has traded recently, though still well below the heights the grocery platform reached when contactless shopping was a necessity rather than a convenience.

## Why It Matters

**Scale is the strategic logic here.** DoorDash already processes a vast number of restaurant orders daily and has built out a logistics infrastructure, Dashers, warehouses, and software, that is expensive to maintain and most efficient when utilisation is high. Adding Instacart's grocery volume to that network raises the density of orders per driver per hour, which is the core unit economics problem every delivery platform has struggled to solve. More stops on the same route means lower cost per delivery. It also means more data: combined purchase behaviour across restaurants and grocery gives the merged entity an advertising and recommendations business that neither could build as convincingly alone.

**The deal reshapes the competitive picture for Uber Eats and Amazon.** Uber Eats competes directly in restaurant delivery and has its own grocery partnerships, while Amazon operates Amazon Fresh and has the fulfilment muscle to be a credible long-term rival. A merged DoorDash-Instacart is bigger, better capitalised, and covers more of the weekly consumer spend that both of those companies have been chasing. Smaller players, regional grocery chains with their own delivery apps, and white-label logistics providers face a much harder environment if the combined company starts bundling and discounting aggressively.

**Regulatory scrutiny is a real variable.** The FTC under recent administrations has shown appetite for challenging consumer-facing tech mergers on market concentration grounds. DoorDash and Instacart do not overlap in an obvious head-to-head way, which gives the deal a cleaner story than, say, a restaurant-to-restaurant combination. But the combined entity's share of the broader on-demand delivery category, and its leverage over grocery retailers who depend on Instacart's technology platform as much as its delivery network, will draw attention. The technology platform angle matters: Instacart supplies software and e-commerce infrastructure to a wide range of grocery chains, and that business sits adjacent to, but distinct from, delivery. Regulators may want to examine whether combining that with DoorDash's market position creates pressure on retailers to consolidate their logistics spend with a single vendor.

  <div class="ttl">Risks to Watch</div>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>Regulatory block or conditions:</strong> The FTC could challenge the deal or extract significant behavioural or structural remedies, particularly around Instacart's grocery-tech platform and its relationships with retail chains who may object to their software supplier being owned by a delivery competitor.</li>
    <li><strong>Integration complexity:</strong> Restaurant logistics and grocery logistics are operationally different. Grocery orders are heavier, more time-sensitive around freshness, and involve more SKUs. Merging two large, distinct fulfilment cultures without degrading service quality is genuinely hard.</li>
    <li><strong>Consumer price sensitivity:</strong> Delivery fees and tip fatigue are already suppressing order frequency across the category. If the combined company raises prices to service deal debt or hit profitability targets, it risks accelerating the return to in-store shopping.</li>
  </ul>
