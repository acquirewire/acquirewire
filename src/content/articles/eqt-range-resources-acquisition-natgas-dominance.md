## What Happened

EQT Corporation is pursuing an acquisition of Range Resources, according to Reuters, in a move that would consolidate two of the largest Appalachian Basin natural gas producers under a single roof. Financial terms have not been disclosed, and the deal remains at an early stage, but the strategic logic is not subtle: EQT already bills itself as the biggest US natural gas producer by volume, and absorbing Range would extend that lead materially, adding Range's substantial acreage in southwest Pennsylvania's Marcellus Shale to EQT's existing footprint.

The approach lands at an unusual moment for the US gas market. Spot prices have recovered from their 2023-2024 lows, LNG export capacity is expanding rapidly along the Gulf Coast, and data-centre power demand is injecting a new, structurally durable source of domestic consumption into the outlook. Producers that control large, low-cost Appalachian reserves are suddenly in a stronger negotiating position than they were two years ago.

## Why It Matters

**Scale in gas is now a strategic asset, not just an operating advantage.** The US is on track to become the world's largest LNG exporter, and the pipeline from Appalachian wellhead to Gulf Coast export terminal is only as good as the volumes feeding it. A combined EQT-Range entity would control enough acreage and throughput to anchor long-term supply agreements with LNG buyers in Europe and Asia, the kind of contracts that underpin project financing for the next generation of liquefaction facilities. Smaller, subscale producers struggle to compete for that business.

**The timing reflects a broader consolidation wave that has already reshaped US oil.** The Permian Basin went through its own round of mega-mergers in 2023 and 2024, with ExxonMobil buying Pioneer Natural Resources and Chevron chasing Hess. Appalachian gas is following the same script: fewer owners, larger positions, lower per-unit costs. Range Resources has long been seen as a potential consolidation target given its high-quality acreage and relatively modest market capitalisation compared to the reserves it sits on. EQT is simply the most obvious acquirer.

**Regulatory scrutiny is the unavoidable variable.** Combining the two largest producers in a single basin will draw attention from the Department of Justice regardless of the political environment. The key question is whether the DOJ frames the relevant market as Appalachian gas specifically, which would make the concentration numbers look stark, or as US natural gas supply broadly, which gives the deal more room. EQT will need a compelling answer to that framing before any deal is signed.

  <div class="ttl">Risks to Watch</div>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>Antitrust exposure:</strong> A combined entity controlling a dominant share of Marcellus output could face a lengthy DOJ review, with divestitures required to get over the line. Any remedies that carve out Range's best acreage would undermine the deal's core rationale.</li>
    <li><strong>Gas price volatility:</strong> The deal's economics are predicated on a constructive gas price environment. A return to the sub-$2/MMBtu conditions that plagued producers in 2023 and early 2024 would pressure the combined company's ability to service any acquisition debt and justify the premium paid.</li>
    <li><strong>Integration complexity:</strong> EQT has spent several years streamlining its own operations after prior acquisitions. Adding Range's acreage, contracts, and workforce before that process is fully bedded down carries execution risk that the market will price in quickly if early signals disappoint.</li>
  </ul>
