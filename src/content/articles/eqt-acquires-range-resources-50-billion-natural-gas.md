## What Happened

EQT Corporation has agreed to acquire Range Resources in an all-stock merger that values the combined natural gas business at more than $50 billion. Range Resources shareholders will receive EQT stock, making this a paper deal rather than a cash takeout, which tells you something about how both boards view the near-term commodity price environment: bullish enough to want exposure, cautious enough to preserve liquidity. The transaction creates what EQT is calling, with some justification, America's largest natural gas producer by volume, anchored in the Appalachian Basin.

Range Resources stock jumped sharply on the news. EQT shares moved more modestly, the typical pattern when an acquirer pays in equity and the market spends time doing the dilution arithmetic.

## Why It Matters

**The AI power story is the engine here.** Data-centre operators, hyperscalers, and the utilities feeding them are locking in long-term gas supply because intermittent renewables cannot yet carry the baseload those facilities demand around the clock. EQT is positioning this deal as a direct play on that structural shift: more scale, lower per-unit costs, and the balance-sheet depth to sign the kinds of long-term offtake contracts that power buyers want. That logic is coherent. It is also the same logic every gas producer is pitching right now, so the question is whether EQT's cost curve is genuinely lower than peers or whether the AI demand story is doing heavy lifting for a deal that is partly about fixing Range's leverage.

**Appalachian consolidation has been building for years.** EQT has already absorbed significant acreage through prior transactions and has been the loudest voice arguing that the basin needs fewer, larger operators to be internationally competitive, particularly against LNG-exporting rivals. A combined EQT-Range would have a production and reserve base that makes it hard to ignore in any serious LNG supply conversation. That scale could also give the merged company pricing power with midstream operators, which is where a meaningful chunk of per-unit cost sits.

**The all-stock structure shifts risk onto Range shareholders.** If EQT's shares underperform between signing and close, Range holders absorb that loss with no cash floor. The deal also depends on Range shareholders approving the exchange ratio, and any deterioration in EQT's outlook, whether from gas prices, regulatory friction, or execution risk on cost synergies, could motivate dissent. Antitrust scrutiny is a genuine variable too. The FTC under the current administration has shown willingness to look hard at energy consolidation, and a deal that explicitly creates the country's dominant gas producer is not going to sail through without a thorough review.
